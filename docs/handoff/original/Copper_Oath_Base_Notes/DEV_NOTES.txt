launcher save-copy or legacy-save regression checks for normal version builds.

SCREEN SWITCH TIMES (v0.014; Douglas playtest: "pretty long loading screen between menus. and map screen to town screen")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured headless on this laptop, Large world (68 towns), one year of the years before (26.5 MB save), with
tests/diag_screen_load.gd (DIAG_CACHE=1 keeps the world between runs; DIAG_SAVE_PARTS=1 splits the save). Each time is
the switch plus the new screen's first frame. Before -> after:
  - map -> town:              2,450-2,920 ms -> ~800 ms   (the town autosave was 2.0-2.3 s of it)
  - town -> map (Leave):        ~2,300+ ms -> ~760 ms     (autosave again, then the map redrew every tree and peak)
  - company/records -> town:  2,450-2,490 ms -> ~130 ms   (the town saved again on every return)
  - company -> map:             230-250 ms -> 20-30 ms    (map decor redraw)
  - company tab switch:           72-87 ms -> 3-12 ms
  - first records open:           ~750 ms -> ~100 ms; first town open had ~530 ms of scene/script load, first company
    ~180 ms, first map ~200 ms: now loaded in the background while the title shows
  - title, new game, settings: already 2-40 ms (unchanged)
The causes and fixes:
  1. Autosave on the town screen (save_game in town.gd _ready, so on arrival AND on every return from the company or
     records screen, and again on Leave). A Large save is ~26 MB of JSON: the walk that tags units, items and
     positions ~1.0 s, JSON.stringify ~1.2 s, all on the main thread. Now: the walk copies each container natively and
     only replaces non-plain values (~0.65 s), and the JSON is made and written by a worker thread
     (Game.save_game_async: to a .tmp file, then moved over the save; load, save_game, game over and quitting wait for
     it). The town autosaves on arrival from the map ({"arrive": true}) and on Leave, not on coming back from its own
     sub-screens. The map's Save button and the end of the years before use the background save too. The save file is
     the same JSON as before (tests/test_ui_load_times.gd compares the old and new walks on a real world). Loading
     rebuilds in place (no second copy): _deserialize 0.9 s -> 0.55 s.
  2. The map layer's decor (every tree, peak, hill... ~70 chunks) was drawn again in code each time the map screen
     opened (~220 ms here, more on a phone). The textures and decor lists were already cached; the chunk nodes were not.
     Now the chunks are RenderingServer canvas items kept with that cache, drawn once per world and hung under each new
     map screen (map_terrain.gd RSPen records the same draw commands). The ground shader is compiled once too.
  3. Company screen: a tab switch rebuilt the roster list (two health reports per man); now only the sheet. The
     roster card takes one report per man (Game.deploy_block_reason accepts the report).
  4. Each screen's scene was loaded from disk at every switch and its scripts compiled on first use; main.gd now loads
     the map, town, company and records scenes in the background at the title and keeps them.
Left as they are: the remaining ~0.65 s main-thread save walk on town arrival and Leave (it is the volume: ~110k
dictionaries and ~315k arrays in the towns' registers, families and herds); the first map open of a new or loaded
world (~0.5 s: building the ground textures and decor lists, once); the town Market tab (~75 ms, one card per item).
Options if the remaining walk still feels long on the phone: autosave on Leave only (not on arrival), or a binary
save (var_to_bytes: 0.4 s but a 46 MB file).
Gate: tests/test_ui_load_times.gd (default Large world, 20 days before): map -> town and town -> map under 1.5 s,
company/records under 0.6 s, a company tab under 60 ms (measured 0.6 s, 0.6 s, under 0.1 s, 13 ms).

STANDING DESIGN DECISIONS (do not relitigate without Douglas)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- No HP. Tissue layers only. Dumb, size-weighted targeting.
- Our own weighted combat system, DF as inspiration. Weights stay; no outcome tuning toward win-rate targets.
- Not invincible = a non-zero chance of losing. Hit floor 0.01% per swing (was 5%).
- Per-weapon air drag stays (small, on top of swing inertia).
- Randomness only at named steps; deterministic physics in between.
- Attribute scale 200-4000, human median 1000; DF ceiling rule with talent stars.
- Player is the mercenary captain, never in battle. Game over when the company is gone. Officer permadeath.
- Monsters/megabeasts never rout. Hydra = epic, dragon = legendary (breathes fire, no wings).
- Economy is real: goods and gold only move by production, use, trade, loss or raids.
- Every non-monster force belongs to a home town and faction.
- Orcs and goblins start hostile (standing -60) to a human company (the default band); other bands start normal. Armies are drafted only from real people of
  their town; a peace holds a 60-120 day truce; militia sally only at 1.25x; the map is 1.5x (war picks).
- Battles: generic animation; detail lives in the combat log. CHANGED v0.014 (Douglas 22:51 "more gore-heavy"):
  blood sprays, pools, drip trails, arterial spurts, wound marks on the sprites and severed parts on the ground are ON
  by default (Settings > Gore: Full; Reduced for a phone build), all driven by the real wounds and bleeding. The
  remaining special animations (parts flying off in an arc, stuck weapons, wrestling) stay behind Settings toggles,
  off by default. Campaign-map sprites static and small.
- Gear breaks for real (v0.014, Douglas 22:52-22:58): bends and dents cost quality steps (bent back, never a repair
  to full); snapped, cracked, split or shattered = scrap of its metal at the battle's end. No per-metal multipliers.
- Caravans move physical goods; the player may raid them and loot the actual goods.
- Tests headless only, standard Godot build only; never open windows; full suite green before any release.
