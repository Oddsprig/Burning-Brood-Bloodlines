WORLD AND FACTION VIEWS PART 1 of 2 (Douglas 01:15: "a selector up there; you can even view the whole world and more tabs"). Same frame, rules and sorting as the town screen spec (every number from the sim, sort any column, tap any name/town/force to open it).

1. THE SELECTOR (top bar dropdown, big)
- Entries in order: The whole world; each faction (crest + name); then every town grouped under its faction. Picking a town opens the town screen; picking a faction or the world opens the views below. DEFAULT (Douglas 01:16): opening the screen while in a town selects THAT town; outside a town, the last town the company visited. The player can then switch to his faction, any other faction, any town or the whole world. With many towns: a search box at the top of the list and the current town first.
- Every town row, faction row or force anywhere on these views is tappable and jumps to its own screen.

2. WHOLE WORLD tabs: Towns, Factions, Rulers, On the move, Goods, Food flows, Fights, Mines & land, Merchants, Census.
- Header line (all tabs): day + season, towns, people, factions at war (pairs), forces on the map.

Towns
- Header: towns, people, average food days, towns short of food / starving, caravans on the road, this season's harvest factor (real weather).
- ASYMMETRY AT A GLANCE (Douglas 01:18): a header line of real spreads across towns, readout only, never a target: food price dearest/cheapest, metal price, arms price, wealth per person, food days, hardship %. Each spread names its two towns.
- Row also carries price columns vs the world average (x1.00 = average) for Food, Drink, Metal, Arms, so sorting by one column shows who is rich in what and who is short.
- Row: town, kind (village / town / city / capital, from its real size and seat), faction, race, LIVES ON (its biggest earners by real output: trade, iron, timber, grain...), MAKES (its top 3 real products and specialties), people, food days, granary days, treasury, families in hardship %, garrison + militia, sick now, health (thriving / steady / struggling / starving from the real food, hardship and treasury trends).

Factions (reference Counties)
- Row: faction, head (person), towns, people, treasury, tax in / army pay out per day, days of pay left, soldiers paid, rank tree size, at war with / allied with / at peace with.
- Detail: relations with every faction (value, last change and its real cause: raid, treaty, war declared), war chest chart, armies in the field (tap), arms contracts.

Rulers (reference Counties; Douglas 01:19: leaders have different effects)
- NEW SIM: every faction head and town head is a register person with age and traits (the existing 13 traits plus what he cares most for). His town's RULES are rolled from his traits and the race culture row, and re-rolled when a new head takes over: tax kind and rate (coin % / share in kind), loan interest and how hard defaults are pursued, labour duty (days a year per family on roads and walls, worked or paid off in coin), militia call-ups per year, extra levies (rare / hard years / often), poorhouse and relief (open / mean), granary target, armory buying, wages for the garrison. Faction heads' traits weight the existing diplomacy roll (greedy: raids and war for gain; cautious: peace; proud: slow to make peace).
- Rulers card: faction head and the heads above the player's town: name, age, traits, cares most for.
- Row per town: seat, head (tap), race, rules in plain words ("coin 0.8%, grain 1/20, levies in hard years, relief mean"), towns under him, people, granary days. Detail: when the rules last changed and why (new head).
- WHAT EACH TOWN ASKS card: one plain sentence per town, head's name and traits first, e.g. "Ashford (Reeve Edric Harrow, greedy, harsh): coin on houses 0.9 in a hundred of their worth a year, a fifteenth of every harvest in kind for the granary; 7 days' labour duty a year on the roads, worked; extra levies often; the militia called up 3 times a year." Built from the live rule values, never stock text.
- Test: same world seed with two different heads in one town gives different rules, and the town's numbers diverge from them (tax take, poorhouse count, militia size).
