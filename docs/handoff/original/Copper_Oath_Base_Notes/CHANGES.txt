Militia and caravan guard kit (Douglas 23:02: "town armories are not desiring enough armor and arms... some caravans
get naked people garding them").
  - The armory's target now covers the whole militia: every armour or weapon slot a militiaman lacks (optional ones
    too) plus the kit for one caravan's guards, on top of the old spare. Before it was only a quarter spare of what the
    town's forces should carry, so a militia that had lost or never had its helms was nobody's want.
  - The head pays for arms from the treasury above what the militia's pay needs, not a tenth of whatever is in the
    chest at each look; what he cannot afford goes into the monthly budget, so the house tax surcharge rises for it.
  - The militia gets its kit, optional slots included, right after he buys it.
  - Caravan guards are kitted from the town's armory first (then the stalls) before the caravan leaves, and their kit
    goes back to the armory when they come home. Before, they only bought off the stalls (a quarter of the stalls for
    the whole caravan, sold back at 70%), so most went out with no armour at all.
  - When the stalls are full, the weaponsmith and armourer still make what the armory is short of, and the head buys
    those pieces straight from them.
  - The town's Economy tab and Records show an armory log: why the militia or a caravan's guards went out short (the
    treasury cannot pay, or nothing on the stalls and the smith has nobody working).
  - Measured on a Normal world (seed 2026, the five years before): caravan guards with body armour 5% -> 56%, a helm
    10% -> 79%, a weapon 42% -> 92%. Militia moved a little (body armour 52% -> 60%, helm 72% -> 79%, weapon 88% ->
    90%; runs vary by a few points): most towns' smiths have
    nobody working by then, so there is little to buy (see DEV_NOTES).
Legendary items and gems (docs/specs/next/03-legendary-items.md, Douglas 22:31-23:00).
  - Item quality refit: a masterwork is 1.4% a piece at smith skill 10 and 25% at skill 15, x1.78 a skill point
    between (2.5%, 4.4%, 7.9%, 14%) and below; the lower qualities moved down with it (a skill-15 smith used to make
    45% masterworks).
  - Legendary, a seventh quality above masterful (quality value 3.0 against masterwork's 2.0, same physics): a real
    smith or bowyer of a town's forge (its keeper or staff) makes one on 1 in 25 of his masterworks (1% a piece at
    skill 15), at most 5 in his life. It needs a real gem from the market (or bought from a family that keeps one);
    without one it stays a masterwork. Militia kit, the opening stock and other rolls with no named smith never make
    one, and the player cannot order one.
  - Each is unique and named ("Oathbreaker, legendary copper sword"), has its gem set in the pommel, socket, boss,
    brow or clasp (shown in its icon and its description), is worth twenty masterworks plus its gem, and keeps a
    history: maker, every carrier, every change of hands, its kills.
  - Never lost: when its carrier falls the winners always take it (player or AI; an AI winner puts it on its best
    fitting fighter); beasts take nothing, so the nearest town picks it up. Never scrapped, sold off with a hoard,
    broken up for study or lost with a caravan, and battle gear damage never bends, snaps, chips or cracks one. The town that lost it remembers it.
  - Shown on the unit card, in Records (World > Fights > Legendary arms, a page each; a town's lost pieces; the
    maker's page and life history; his recruit story).
  - Gems are real goods (rock crystal, amethyst, garnet, topaz, sapphire, ruby, emerald). No gem mines: every basket
    of ore has a 0.2% chance of a gem pocket, the stone by the rock (hilly ground: garnet, sapphire, ruby; tin
    country: topaz, emerald). Middling and rich families buy stones as stored wealth from their spare money (the rich
    the dearest), and a family that cannot pay for food sells its stones before it pawns anything.

v0.010  Bronze and Barley: the economy rebuilt on the Barge rules
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Money is bronze coins now. Every price, wage, purse, treasury, contract and loot sum is counted in coins:
    1 old gold piece = 20 coins, so a labourer earns about 100 coins a day, a grain meal costs about 4, a spear
    hundreds. The company starts with 12,000 coins; its men's wages are 80 to 240 a day. The loot rules are
    unchanged: 5% of a dead man's household purse (10% if he was the last of his house), never more than 500.
  - Coins are real: they are cast at mints in the realms' seats and mining towns from real copper and tin bars
    by real minters, they wear out and get lost (a little every day), and smiths melt them when the metal is worth
    more than the coin. No coin appears from nothing anywhere in the world any more.
  - Prices follow what people can pay: each good has its own kind (staples, fresh food, fuel, cloth, tools...)
    with its own normal stock and swing; prices step toward their mark a little each day. Each realm has a price
    level that rises when coin is plentiful and falls when it is scarce; a town short of coin hires less and buys
    less.
  - Food rots by the weather: meat and fruit spoil fast in the hot south and keep in the cold north; grain keeps.
    Grain is cut twice a year (early and autumn grain) and some years are bad harvests, some good.
  - Every household shops for itself, by class (poor, middling, rich): its staple first (by its race's taste),
    then firewood (the poor gather some), then meat, fruit, drink, clothes and tools as it can afford.
  - Hunger is per family: a family with food in its larder is fed whatever the town's state. Hunger runs in
    stages from Short to Collapse; hungry families work less, the worst-off lose people to hunger (real people,
    the weakest first), and hungry families may leave for another town of their realm with their purse and goods.
    Towns with empty houses and better pay draw families in.
  - Work: each town has a day wage set by how much work there is for how many hands. Farms hire their field hands
    first, at a little more than the day wage, so the land gets worked; children of 10-14 and the old help on the
    family land; farm families plough with oxen. Trades pay by a pay table (a smith about 110, a weaver about 95).
  - Soldiers of every race are paid in coins by rank (recruit 70, soldier 100, sergeant 150 ... commander 500)
    plus 5 a day for each grade of skill with their weapon (a Legendary footman earns 150). New men get a
    sign-on purse, every fight pays a day's wage and a win a day more. Unpaid field soldiers go home after 3 days;
    an unpaid militia loses men day by day.
  - Towns collect a monthly service money by class and a house and land tax, take a share of every harvest
    (in grain or coin, by the ruler's temper), pay their garrison only when they can (else they owe it), raise
    the levy when short, and in a crisis borrow from the rich or debase the coin. The granary sells cheap grain in
    dear times, buys when grain is cheap, rations, and runs a gruel kitchen for the hungry poor.
  - Poor families rent their houses from the town's rich; families short of coin pawn their goods before they
    borrow; the head's loans carry the same terms in every realm.
  - The years before (seed 2026, five years): the world no longer empties. Before this version 8,401 people fell
    to 313 (most walked off the map hungry, 38% of the land lay idle from the first day). Now, with the land
    map, sickness, the census and the war picks in the same world, 8,063 people end the five years at 6,582:
    84-86% of the land worked every year, about one household in eleven hungry by year 5, nobody lost off the
    map (families who leave go to another town, 669 people over the five years), no soldier made up. Deaths
    still outrun births (1,871 against 390: battle 629, sickness 578, old age 372, starved 280): numbers for
    Douglas before v0.010 ships. The years before take about 47 s on the dev laptop (was about 30 s for a
    world that died).
  - Town size is one world-gen number (EconGen.TOWN_SCALE: families and fields together); it stays at 1.
  - The land map (Town tab -> "Land map"): every town's land is now plots of about 5 acres, each a real record,
    drawn as a square tile with its own bright sprite: wheat fields that are sown, growing, ripe, stubble and bare
    with the seasons, orchards, vineyards, pasture with the herd that grazes it, dense, thinned, felled and
    regrowing woods, homes in the town's own style (timber houses, longhouses, orc hide tents, goblin burrows, elf

(part of v0.006)  Economy spec 1: the real town economy, core with families  (docs/specs/economy-1-town.md)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rule zero: goods are integer counts in a town market, a barn, the granary, a household pool, a caravan, a force or
the company; gold sits in market and business tills, family purses, treasuries, faction coffers, caravan purses and
the company. Nothing appears except by production and nothing leaves except by use, wear, spoilage, loss or raids.
Code: campaign/econ_gen.gd (world gen), families.gd (records, weekly turn), businesses.gd (rows, daily production),
town_econ.gd (daily pass, prices, supply, arms stock, town/faction turns, migration), people.gd (people in forces,
wages home), goods.gd (read side); data: data/economy.json (goods, needs, climates, crops, herds, businesses, culture
rows incl. spec-2 rank tables, specialties, know-how, tax). Town screen: new "Town" tab + family records.
  - World gen: climate from latitude (cold north, hot south), 0-3 ore deposits (copper common, tin rare), crops and
    herds by climate with variety labels (barley ale, cider, mutton...), 1-2 specialties (x2 rate, +1 quality tier),
    culture row, 50-150 families (by size, cap 150) with land, herds, homes, purses; family-owned businesses; a named
    head with a family record; opening stocks ("pre-history").
  - Daily town pass (work queue, one job per map frame): fields harvest over the autumn into the barns (5% grain
    tax in kind to the granary), herds daily (autumn slaughter peak); the market buys from the barns (landholders
    paid per acre/head through a running total); businesses run their most wanted feasible recipe (tools wear,
    inputs bought, output sold to the market; smiths spend bars, no bars = idle; know-how gates bronze); households
    eat, drink, burn firewood and wear out goods from the household pool; spoilage; prices.
  - Prices = the sum of the actors' orders against stock (Douglas): families order to their own targets (30 days
    food, 14 drink, 14 firewood, worn goods as they wear), businesses order inputs (only businesses that run: no
    smelter = no ore demand), the head orders for the granary. price = base x clamp(sqrt(need/stock), 0.5, 3.0),
    need = smoothed daily orders x 14 days; surplus prices below base, shortage above. Weekly reason line per good.
  - Weekly family turn (1/7 of families a day): wages from employers, owner profits and top-ups, stores counted
    down (hunger), seasonal tax, loans from the head, the land loop (loan -> confiscation after 3 weeks -> sold to a
    family -> else the town works it), home decay and repair, jobs, life events, restock orders (cleared once).
  - Weekly town turn: collectors (missing ones leak tax), market tax, estate, land and business sales, town wages,
    public works when the treasury is flush, granary (buys cheap, opens when food is dear), new head, trade plan.
  - Weekly faction turn: towns send 10% above reserve; 25% of the coffer returns to the poorest towns until army pay
    (spec 2); low coffers push toward peace, food/metal shortage toward a pact with a supplier or war on a weak holder.
  - Caravans (spec 9): owned by the home town's merchants, purse from the treasury; buy real surplus at origin price,
    sell at the destination price (unsold stays on the wagons), buy the home town's needs, return home; carry
    bronze know-how; crew (guards + 2 drivers) are people of town families paid daily by the owner; an owner who
    cannot pay sails no caravan. Raids take 60% of the cargo and the purse, the rest is lost; town standing -10
    (-20 with witnesses), faction -12 (-6 more with witnesses).
  - People in forces: militiamen and caravan crews are family members in service (paid into family purses, the
    dead leave the family); company wages go man by man (officers first) into family purses while the gold lasts,
    gold never below 0, unpaid days counted and shown ("N men unpaid"); company men get family records.
  - Shops sell the town's real arms stock (smith output); faction bands raised in the campaign, new militiamen and
    caravan guards take their gear from home stock (treasury pays). Recruits come mostly from struggling rows, with a
    real-state backstory line, cheaper in hard times; hiring takes the person out of the family.
  - Supply levels (well / short / starving: >14 / 3-14 / <3 days of food) feed the food spec at battle spawn.
  - Loot hooks: dead men carry min(5% of the household purse, 500) (last of the house: 10% to the looter, the rest
    to the faction); extinct houses pass property to the town head, cash to the faction; battle scrap goes into the
    nearest town's stock (smelters remelt it). Contract pay comes out of the employer town's treasury.
  - Old saves: an economy is generated per town (old shop Items become the arms stock, old food/ale go to market).

  - Pain, bleeding, arteries, suffocation, severed limbs and heads, lost grip and stance, eyes.
  - Morale, rout and rally; rage. Stuck weapons (twist, pull out, let go). Unarmed fallback: punch,

  - Skill and attribute growth from battle.
  - Per-unit combat log: a short story line, one tap for what happened to each tissue, a second tap
    for the numbers behind it. Text built only from what the sim actually did; many variants.
  - Battle terrain: open, rough and blocked tiles; corridor maps for chokes.

Screens
  - Battle: layered sprites built from body parts (missing limbs show), walk/swing/hit/fall, a feed of
