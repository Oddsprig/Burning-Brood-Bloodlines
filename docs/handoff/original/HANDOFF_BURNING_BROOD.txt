BURNING BROOD: BLOODLINES
Separate game handoff from Copper Oath v0.046
Prepared 2026-10-10. Working title; renaming later is fine.

1. CONFIRMED USER DIRECTION
The player is a family ruling one of the cities. The same kind of campaign map
and battles remain, along with the full economy. The central experience is
managing dragons, their riders and troops, and fighting wars with them.
Family politics, settlement politics within a city, politics between
settlements/factions, and marriage alliances become the main campaign themes.
CK3/AGOT is a reference for the feel of dynastic politics and dragon warfare.
Build this game's own setting and institutions.
The presentation moves toward green, purple and dark tones. It must organize
information into useful cards, columns and clear actions; avoid prose walls.
The title should suit a mobile game. Mobile readability and touch controls
should shape the new UI from the start.
Copper Oath continues as a separate game.

Everything below is a proposed implementation plan unless specifically
identified as an existing system or confirmed user rule. No dates, final
balance numbers, dragon-bond laws or succession laws have been agreed.

2. THE CAMPAIGN LOOP
Manage the ruling family, heirs, court and relationships.
Govern the city through the existing treasury, households, trades and services.
Keep dragons fed and fit, raise their young, choose riders and organize troops.
Arrange marriages and agreements with other houses, cities and factions.
Choose wars, deploy actual forces on the campaign map and fight tactical battles.
Apply the real casualties, injuries, costs and political consequences.
Continue through leadership succession and the next generation.

An army defeat does not automatically end the dynasty. Leadership, military
command, dragon ownership and dragon-rider bonds are separate relationships.
A ruler or heir may serve as a rider if the eventual rules allow it. Do not
silently inherit Copper Oath's rule that the player captain never fights.

3. KEEP AND EXTEND THESE SYSTEMS
Economy: household property/purses, actual plots and livestock, work and wages,
business ownership/keepers, market goods and real item provenance, taxes,
treasuries, debt, trade, named food, supply, housing, sanitation and sickness.
A new dragon expense must debit a real payer and consume real supplies. Do not
replace town economies with abstract city income or duplicate the market.
House money, city treasury and faction money retain separate ownership and
explicit transfers. Ruling a city does not make every citizen's goods yours.

Population: existing adults/children/dead records, birth dates, spouses,
parents, histories and live Unit links. A noble house is political/dynastic
metadata tied to these people and households. It may span several households
and towns; an economic household and a dynasty are different concepts.
Do not create duplicate family trees, extra populations or invented rulers.

Politics: towns already have head family/name/title records and culture-based
capital/town titles. Faction ruler policies, diplomacy and military leadership
also exist. Persistent person-linked officeholding and fuller succession are
unfinished. Resolve the existing head to an actual registered person and extend
the office. Never assume a generated head name already proves that identity.
Make city/faction ruling families unmistakable in the register and family page.

Military: existing armies, militia/guards, officer hierarchy, supply, equipment
and campaign movement. Mobilization uses actual people and releases their real
civilian work. Demobilization and casualties reconcile the same service records.
Garrisons buy arms/armour from the open market. Existing reserves target 2.5
times supported troop equipment beyond issued kit; internal distribution checks
every 10 days within the same faction. Preserve that baseline during the split.
Trade and internal military distribution remain distinct transactions.

Combat: BattleSim's existing timed tactical grid, initiative, terrain,
weapons/armour, material/tissue/body-part damage, blood loss, wounds, skill
learning, morale/rout, aftermath and lasting injuries. Keep this combat style.
Dragons and riders join this simulation through extensions. They do not use a
second battle resolver or a separate hit-point abstraction.
Creature infrastructure exists, but the current creature data contains wolf,
bear and boar only. Dragon bodies, dragon art, flight, breath attacks and
ridden-dragon mechanics are new work, not shipped v0.046 features.

Presentation: readable Market/Provisions/Recruit/Healer/Contracts/Town and
Town/World/Faction Records already exist. Reuse tables, links, filters, sorting,
details and original transactions. Carry those improvements into the new UI.

4. DRAGON MANAGEMENT - PROPOSED SCOPE
Each dragon is an individual: stable identity, name, parents/offspring, age,
growth, physical traits, temperament, owner/custodian, location, health and
history. Eggs and juveniles develop into adults; stages and rates are undecided.
Add feeding, care, shelter, breeding/hatching and injuries in manageable steps.
Show real daily supply demand and upkeep before the player commits to more
dragons. Obtain food from the existing food/goods/herd economy.
Keep ownership, rider bond, military assignment and current location separate.
A marriage transfers only the assets or rights actually written into its terms;
it does not automatically transfer a dragon or force a new rider bond.

Riders must be actual people. Qualification, training, attempted bonding and
failed bonds need explicit rules. A proposed first model permits one active
rider per dragon and one active dragon per rider; this is not a settled law.
Heritage may matter if designed later, but do not assume that heirs become
riders, inherit a bond or replace a dead rider automatically.
Resolve rider death, dragon death, capture, departure and failed succession
once against the true identities. An unbonded dragon can remain alive and owned.

5. POLITICS AND MARRIAGES - PROPOSED SCOPE
City: ruling office, council/court roles, prominent households, interests,
loyalty, grievances, patronage and competing claims. Economic pressures should
feed political opinions instead of producing unrelated random events.
Realm: rulers, factions, subject/independent settlements, diplomatic agreements,
war demands, obligations and succession disputes. Start with bounded systems;
full intrigue, rebellions, conquest and castle sieges are later expansions.
Family: stable dynasty membership, heir rules, branches, claims and office history.
Royal/noble households must not disappear or be regenerated when a ruler dies.

Marriages: extend existing same-town civilian marriage and kinship into real
negotiated agreements across cities/houses. Show eligible adults, both parties,
relationship/claims, residence, dynasty membership, optional dowry, obligations
and intended alliance effect before acceptance. Choose cultural succession and
marriage rules at that milestone.
Preserve spouse/parent/history/health/property links through moves between
households and towns. Dowries/property transfers must conserve real assets.
Civilian marriages continue; political matching must not turn every household
into a noble house or block the natural population system.
An alliance is an explicit political agreement linked to the marriage; it needs
defined duration and breach/death/succession rules, not a permanent free bonus.

Remember real events: deaths, broken commitments, service and harm to relatives.
The user previously requested memory of who killed family members and visible
kinship to prior recruits. Extend actual history/kinship for political memory.
Descendants are not forced into recruitment or dragon-rider service.

6. PROPOSED VERSION MAP - NEW GAME'S OWN NUMBERING
Inherited code baseline: Copper Oath v0.046. First new-game build: v0.001.
These are dependency-ordered milestones, not delivery dates or authorization
to implement everything at once. Split a milestone if needed.

v0.001 - Separate identity and ruler foundation
Own project/user-data/launcher; actual ruling family/person and starting city;
clear offices and accounts; dark green/purple UI shell. Existing map, economy
and troop battle still work. No dynasty game-over on a mere troop wipe.

v0.002 - Dynasty and city politics
Persistent offices/succession, heirs, house membership, visible ruling families,
a small useful court/council and local interests tied to the real economy.
Leadership survives movement and ordinary army losses.

v0.003 - Marriages and external politics
Cross-city political marriage proposals/terms, correct person moves, dowries,
alliances/obligations and house/faction relations. Show reasons and consequences.
A single accepted agreement changes assets and relations exactly once.

v0.004 - Dragons and the first rider bond
Individual dragons and eggs, parentage, care/food/upkeep, growth and health;
one initially approved bond model, real riders and a clear dragon-management UI.
Finish a playable small roster before adding many breeds or rare powers.

v0.005 - Dragon/rider tactical battles
Extend existing body/attack/scheduler/terrain/render systems for dragons,
rider targeting, grounded movement and injury persistence. Then add flight and
breath with explicit reach, range, terrain and damage rules. Each combatant's
actual result returns to the same campaign identity.

v0.006 - Dragon wars and campaign integration
Army deployment/supply, campaign dragon movement, AI rulers/riders, bounded
war goals/settlement control and political aftermath. Keep ordinary troops
valuable. Victory, losses and ruler death affect actual offices and relationships.

v0.007 - First cohesive mobile-oriented development build
Finish the first-city experience, family/dragon/war explanations, touch layouts,
readable battle controls, current-build saving and short release checks.
Android/iOS export, packaging, performance budgets and monetization require
their own scoped work; this handoff does not claim a mobile port is finished.

Later: richer succession crises/plots, sieges and keeps, additional dragons,
more monsters, wider worlds, improved people/portraits/weapons. New world size
and content must preserve real settlement economies. Carry the existing art
archive requirement into each replacement.

their town. A name or bare PID is not a global identity. Cross-town political

duplicate Units for every civilian merely to create a political family tree.

8. DESIGN DECISIONS TO RESOLVE AT THEIR MILESTONE
Starting house/city selection, eligible cultures and opening dragon access.
Succession law, house membership after marriage and office/claim inheritance.
Who can bond, dragon custody versus ownership and rider training rules.
Dragon stages, growth, feeding, eggs/breeding, rarity and starting roster.
Flight, collision/footprint, breath/fire injuries and dragon-vs-dragon combat.
War goals, settlement occupation and when full sieges enter the roadmap.
Portrait/landscape mobile layout and eventual platform/export targets.

Use practical stated defaults to build a reviewable small milestone; ask the
user about choices that materially change their intended game. Keep all named
examples as examples, never as special-case production rules.
