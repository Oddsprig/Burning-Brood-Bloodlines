   Keep the same animations, pose names, frame order/count, timing, facing,

   with animation/anchor metadata and a clear old-to-new asset mapping.
   The archive is part of every replacement, not a one-time backup at the end.
   Exact Desktop folder and visual style can be settled when this is scheduled.

2. Weapon and item visuals: improve both inventory/market icons and the actual
   weapons carried by animated people. Keep the icons, portraits and battle
   sprites visually consistent. Preserve recognisable weapon types, material,
   quality and legendary marks, equipment slots and existing combat behaviour.
   Use the same legacy-export workflow for every replaced weapon/item visual.
   Compare old/new appearances across the existing art preview's poses,
   equipment combinations and portrait views before shipping replacements.

   The existing later-wave plans are candidate content, not new commitments:

   current Normal/Large/Huge options. Decide whether to enlarge those settings
   or add another size; dimensions, settlement counts and travel pacing are
   still open. Retain the full economy in every settlement and account for
   generation/simulation performance when choosing the expansion.

   head with an appropriate title (reeve, mayor, baron, etc. by settlement and

   Town heads and cultural titles already exist, and capital heads have realm
   titles. Stable person-linked political records and fuller succession remain

   generating a second unrelated set of leaders.
   Clearly mark the ruling family in the register and household record, with
   the officeholder, title and settlement/faction governed; do not rely only
   on a shared surname or a link buried in town details.

6. Settlement keeps/castles: each settlement should have an actual, identifiable

   Extend those buildings and their presentation; plan what physical layout,

7. A mercenary fort: a company home the player can build and add to, where the
   band can stay between expeditions. Treat it as a company base and extend
   existing camp, building, inventory and company systems. Design construction
   costs, land/access and upgrades before implementation. Quarters, storage,
   armory, training and an infirmary are possible modules, not a fixed list.

WHAT THE GAME IS
----------------
A mercenary-company auto-battler for phones (tested on PC first). Battle Brothers-style campaign and company;
Dwarf Fortress-style combat (no hit points: tissue layers, wounds, bleeding, pain), fought in short ticks.
You are the captain and never fight; the game ends when your company is gone. The world runs on its own:
real people in real families, a real economy in bronze coins, wars, caravans, monsters, sickness.

Testing: Claude runs the relevant tests and a launch check before each release; TO_TEST.txt is the playtest list.
(Turned back on 2026-10-06 after untested builds shipped with start-screen and screen bugs.)

NEXT, IN ORDER (all confirmed by Douglas)
-----------------------------------------
  1. Healing: real treatment (bandages, stitching, splints); untreated wounds turn bad; fighting hurt;
     lasting damage (limps, lost eyes); rest; early treatment works best. Nerves follow real biology, not
     permanent by default: a bruised or stretched nerve recovers over weeks; a cut nerve regrows slowly over
     months, often only partly, by how bad the cut is, age, health and treatment; a cut spinal cord does not come
     back on its own. Nothing is hard-locked as untreatable, so a future treatment (e.g. magic) could restore it.
     A cut nerve has living tissue on both sides, so it reconnects rather than rebuilds; but the far side dies back
     over weeks (its sheath and the muscle stay alive a while) and unused muscle wastes over months, so any
     reconnect, natural or magical, gives back less the longer it waits.
  2. Downed enemies after a battle: finish off, take prisoner, ransom, bounties dead or alive, or release.
  3. Aftermath and retirement: AI battles' dead and wounded hit their real families; veterans retire on a
     pension; a Family tab for each man. Descendants use ordinary recruitment rules, with no special
     recruitment chance or teaching bonus. When encountered, their details identify the actual son,
     daughter, grandchild, etc. relationship to a prior company recruit, even if they never join.
     Real people remember when your company killed their family member; preserve the actual relative
     and company responsibility in personal records. These are future design notes, not v0.037 features.
  4. Wild land on each town's land map first (Douglas 21:21): more room for forest worked by woodcutters, hunting
     ground with game (a real population that breeds and can be overhunted) for hunters, mineral deposits from the
     town's real geology, and so on; real plots and real jobs, one business per production trade. Deposits
     (Douglas 22:26): iron, copper, tin (rare, clustered, so bronze needs trade) and flux stone around the map and
     near cities; mines are plots worked by real miners, and deposits can be worked out.
     Then the economy pieces not built yet (full list in DEV_NOTES "not built"): foreign coin and forgery, funerals,
     mortgages and courts, business closing and reopening, housing values, riots, merchant tolls, ransoms
     and army foraging.
  5. Conquest (later, with an on/off toggle): factions take existing towns in war; the only way villages change
     hands. No new villages are ever founded during play (Douglas). With conquest comes one single neutral
     melting-pot city: ONLY when a whole race has no cities left anywhere do its surviving families go there; while
     even one city of their race remains, they flee to their own race as now. It starts very small (one family
     of each race) with lots of open land and room, so it can take refugees later. It is ruled by a senate
     elected by vote, one representative per race; the first seats are the heads of the founding families.

DECIDED (don't change without Douglas)
--------------------------------------
  - No hit points; tissue layers only. Our own weighted combat, Dwarf Fortress as inspiration; no tuning
    toward win rates.
  - Everyone is a real person in a real family; nothing appears from nowhere (goods, coin or people).
  - The full economy runs in every town, no simplified model.
  - Every sick person is real; sickness may run every second day to save time (limit 8 ms a game day).
  - Town size stays normal (bigger towns tested: no more stable, slower).
  - Tests run headless only and never open windows; relevant tests and a launch check before every release.
